# What to Eat - Product Vision

What to Eat is an AI-powered dining assistant designed to help people with dietary restrictions order food as quickly and confidently as anyone else.

Most people can glance at a menu and order immediately. People with allergies, intolerances, or dietary restrictions often have to spend several minutes analyzing ingredients, asking questions, researching substitutions, and worrying about whether something is safe.

What to Eat removes that friction.

The app scans menus or searches restaurant menus online, compares menu items against a user's personalized Dietary Profile, and instantly recommends what they can safely order, including exactly what modifications to request.

## Core Experience

When users open the app, they land on a clean, modern dashboard with two main actions:

## 1. Scan Menu

Users take photos of a physical menu using their camera.

The AI:

- reads the menu
- identifies ingredients and potential allergens
- compares items against the user's Dietary Profile
- categorizes meals by safety and compatibility

The app then generates easy-to-read order recommendations.

### Categories

**Safe to Order As-Is**

Meals that appear compatible without modifications.

**Safe With Modifications**

Meals that become compatible after edits.

**Likely Unsafe**

Meals that conflict with the user's restrictions.

### Example

**Chicken Rice Bowl**

**Modify Order**

Remove:

- Cheese
- Sour Cream

Confirm:

- Sauce is dairy-free

Substitutions:

- Sub side salad for fries
- Sub corn tortillas for flour tortillas

Notes:

- Ask about shared fryer if gluten-sensitive

Confidence: High

The structure is intentionally fast and scannable:

- no paragraphs
- no complicated explanations
- just actionable ordering instructions

Users should also be able to:

- copy the order
- save the meal
- show the order directly to a server
- use large-text "Server Mode"
- optionally play a voice version aloud

## 2. Search Restaurants

Users can:

- search restaurants by name
- browse nearby restaurants on a map
- explore cuisine types
- filter by dietary compatibility

The app pulls menu data from:

- restaurant websites
- public menu sources
- APIs
- delivery platforms where available

The AI then performs the same analysis it does for scanned menus.

## Dietary Profile ("Dietary DNA")

Every user creates a personalized food profile.

This includes:

- allergies
- intolerances
- dietary lifestyles
- severity level
- cross-contamination sensitivity
- disliked foods
- preferred cuisines
- nutritional goals

Examples:

- Dairy-free
- Gluten-free
- Nut allergy
- Vegan
- Halal
- Low FODMAP
- Keto

The profile powers all recommendations.

## Saved Meals Library

Users can save successful meals for future visits.

Saved entries include:

- restaurant
- meal name
- modifications
- personal rating
- notes
- reorder instructions

Example:

**Chipotle - Chicken Bowl**

Saved Order:

- White rice
- Chicken
- Fajita veggies
- Pico
- Corn salsa
- Guac
- No cheese
- No sour cream

Rating: Five stars

Tags:

- Dairy-free
- Gluten-free

Notes:

"Staff understood modifications easily."

Over time, the app becomes a personalized safe-food database.

## Core Product Philosophy

The app should feel:

- fast
- youthful
- modern
- calming
- confidence-building

Not clinical.

Not overly technical.

The goal is:

> "Order like everybody else. Eat like yourself."

## Important Safety Principle

The app should never guarantee safety.

Instead, recommendations should say:

- "Appears compatible"
- "Please confirm with restaurant staff"
- "Cross-contamination may still be possible"

Especially for severe allergies.

## MVP (Version 1)

Focus on solving one thing extremely well:

Launch Features:

- AI menu analysis
- Safe / Modify / Avoid recommendations
- Save meals

Version 2:

- Restaurant search

## Biggest Competitive Advantage

Most food apps organize food by:

- popularity
- price
- cuisine

What to Eat organizes food by:

- compatibility
- confidence
- clarity
- speed of decision-making

You are not just helping people find food.

You are reducing:

- anxiety
- decision fatigue
- social awkwardness
- ordering friction

That is a very strong emotional product angle.
